Sector Rotation

Sector rotation occurs when institutional investors reallocate money from one industry to another as market cycles shift. Investors sell shares in underperforming or maturing sectors to buy companies in sectors expected to grow faster. This process redistributes market capital without changing the total amount of money in the stock market.

Investors on the EGX might sell real estate shares like Talaat Moustafa to buy banking stocks when domestic interest rates rise sharply.

Treasury Bills (T-Bills)

Treasury bills are short-term debt instruments issued by governments to raise immediate cash from banks and institutions. They are sold at a discount to face value and do not pay periodic interest. Upon maturity, the government pays the full nominal value, providing a guaranteed short-term gain to the holder.

The Central Bank of Egypt auctions 95 billion EGP of T-bills so local commercial banks can invest surplus liquidity safely.

Foreign Exchange Reserves

Foreign exchange reserves are foreign currencies and liquid financial assets managed by a national central bank. These funds serve as a buffer to secure payments for international trade and fulfill foreign debt commitments. A high reserve balance builds confidence in the country's economy and protects the local currency against external shocks.

Egypt's foreign exchange reserves reaching $57.50 billion helps ensure the country can import essential commodities like wheat and oil smoothly.
