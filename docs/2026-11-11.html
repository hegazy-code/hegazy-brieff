Remittances from Egyptians working abroad reached USD 13 billion in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026/2027, according to an official announcement by the Central Bank of Egypt. Foreign currency transfers from workers overseas represent one of Egypt’s essential sources of hard currency, alongside Suez Canal revenues, tourism, and exports. The strong inflow highlights sustained engagement from the Egyptian diaspora and increased reliance on official banking channels rather than informal currency markets. Market data shows the Egyptian pound strengthened slightly against the US dollar, with USD/EGP declining 0.63% to 53.51. In finance, remittances act as a vital balance-of-payments stabilizer, strengthening foreign exchange liquidity in local commercial banks and providing central monetary authorities with greater flexibility to maintain currency stability. Higher dollar inflows lower the country’s current account deficit and reduce default risk premiums on sovereign debt. For investors and businesses operating in Egypt, steady remittance flows improve access to trade credit and import financing, easing foreign currency bottlenecks across key sectors.

High overseas remittances -> Foreign exchange inflows enter Egyptian banks -> Exporters and importers gain access to hard currency while USD/EGP falls 0.63% -> Egypt gains balance-of-payments stability -> Watch Central Bank reserve announcements and foreign exchange liquidity.
