The US dollar strengthened by 2.11% against the Egyptian pound on October 27, rising to EGP 56.15 per dollar according to currency market data. The foreign exchange movement follows reported portfolio capital outflows of nearly $700 million from Egyptian financial markets, driven by heightened regional geopolitical tensions and global market caution. When foreign institutional investors pull capital out of local bond and equity markets, they convert their local Egyptian pound proceeds back into US dollars to repatriate funds. This surge in foreign currency demand puts immediate upward pressure on the USD/EGP exchange rate. The sell-off also spilled over into the Egyptian Exchange (EGX), where the EGX30 benchmark faced significant downward pressure before seeking stabilization. For Egypt, a weaker local currency raises the EGP cost of imported essential commodities like wheat and fuel, which can feed into domestic inflation rates. Furthermore, currency shifts impact corporate earnings for Egyptian firms with dollar-denominated debts or imported raw material costs. Students of finance should closely observe how foreign exchange fluctuations interact with interest rates, foreign reserves, and stock market valuation multiples during periods of capital flight.
