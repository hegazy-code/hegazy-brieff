Global markets experienced a sharp downturn on November 25, 2026, as investors moved away from risky assets. The S&P 500 fell 2.95%, while the technology-heavy Nasdaq dropped 4.35%. This ’risk-off’ sentiment—where investors sell stocks to move into safer assets—was fueled by persistent inflation fears and expectations of further interest rate hikes by the Federal Reserve. A sharp sell-off rocked global financial markets, led by a steep decline in technology equities and mounting anxiety in artificial intelligence debt markets. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite index plunged 4.35%, while the broader S&P 500 fell 2.95% and the US Technology sector dropped 5.16%. In Asia, South Korea’s KOSPI dropped 6.16% and Taiwan fell 4.80%. The downturn was triggered by a rapid reversal in sentiment surrounding AI listings. Shares of AI startup Anthropic fell below their $83 initial public offering (IPO) price within a week of its record Nasdaq listing, sinking 26.5% from its debut and erasing nearly $200 billion in market value. At the same time, fears surrounding AI debt markets intensified despite Jensen Huang’s return as Nvidia CEO, providing little relief to credit markets that have funded massive tech infrastructure expansion. An IPO (Initial Public Offering) is when a private company sells stock to the public on an exchange for the first time. When credit markets shudder, it means lenders fear heavily indebted borrowers may default on their loan payments. This broad retreat from risky assets impairs global capital flows to emerging economies, making foreign portfolio investment in Egyptian treasury bills and stocks more difficult to attract.

Rising AI debt concerns and Anthropic IPO drop -> Investors sell off tech shares, pushing Nasdaq down 4.35% -> Winners: cash holders and defensive utilities; Losers: tech shareholders and AI debt buyers -> Egypt faces reduced foreign portfolio inflows -> Watch US tech earnings reports and corporate debt spreads.

The Central Bank of Egypt (CBE) continues its efforts to regulate the domestic money supply by absorbing excess liquidity. On the latest reporting date, the CBE accepted 243.2 billion EGP in liquidity from 12 participating banks. By withdrawing this cash from the system, the central bank aims to reduce the amount of money circulating in the economy, which can help dampen inflation. For a student of finance, this is a classic example of ’contractionary’ monetary policy.
